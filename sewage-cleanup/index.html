A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Schenectady Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Schenectady, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

First, get everyone, animals included, away from the contaminated rooms. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. Switching off the heating and cooling and the affected circuits is wise, provided you stay clear of the contaminated water while doing it. The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. If materials have to be replaced, full restoration can stretch to 1 to 3 weeks, based on how much was damaged. We prioritize getting your property safe for occupancy as quickly as possible.
A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement.
