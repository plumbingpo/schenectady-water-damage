Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Schenectady

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. Our team in Schenectady finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold Growth

Mold thrives in a wall cavity that is both damp and dark. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

How We Fix It

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Make Targeted Openings

To get at wet cavities we open only specific spots, take out unsalvageable cabinet parts, drywall or insulation, and seal anything moldy in bags so its spores stay out of the rest of the home.

3

Dry the Structure

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.
