Wet Carpet Drying in Schenectady

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Schenectady, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water Quality Drops

Water that started clean does not stay that way. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.
