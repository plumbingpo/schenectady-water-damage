Bathroom Water Damage in Schenectady

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Schenectady homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
