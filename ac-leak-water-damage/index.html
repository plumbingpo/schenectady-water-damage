Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Schenectady

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether mold has started and needs containment and removal.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • Square footage of ceiling, wall and flooring that has to be replaced.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
