Attic Water Damage in Schenectady

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in Schenectady inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water Near Electrical

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.
