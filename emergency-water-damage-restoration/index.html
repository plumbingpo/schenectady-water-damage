Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Wrap plumbing in cold, unheated parts of the house so a hard freeze cannot crack it.
  • Going away longer than a few days? Close the main water valve first.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Aging supply lines to toilets, faucets and washing machines should be swapped out before one fails.
  • Keep the number of an emergency restoration company saved in your phone before you need it.
  • Put leak alarms beneath sinks and beside the dishwasher, washer and water heater.

Why Emergency Water Damage Restoration Is Not a DIY Job

Mopping the puddle in front of you is rarely the real challenge during an emergency. What takes skill is figuring out where everything else has soaked in. Baseboards, wall cavities and the space beneath flooring all take on water in minutes. With a thermal imaging camera and moisture meters we locate that hidden water and treat it the same night, before it turns up weeks later as an odor or a stain.

Truck-mounted extractors, commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers matched to the room come with the crew, and no household keeps those around. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. That record helps when you file a claim. From the first night to a dry house, a single crew stays on your job.

Signs It Is Time for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Trapped water overhead has made part of the ceiling bulge or sag.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.
  • Floor-level power strips, plugged-in appliances or the breaker panel are now wet.
  • The house was flooded when you walked in, with no telling how many hours it had been going.
  • Wet carpet, wood flooring or wallboard has stayed saturated longer than a few hours.
  • Water is still flowing and you cannot find or reach the shutoff valve.
